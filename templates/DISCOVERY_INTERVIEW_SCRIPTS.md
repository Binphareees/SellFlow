# Discovery Interview Scripts (Field Version)

**Scope:** Three personas — Business Owners, Suppliers / Product Owners, Emerging Vendors / Resellers.
**Basis:** SellFlow PRD v0.2, Section 21.1 (three-sided model: seller, supplier, reseller). *Note: the PRD itself is not in this repo — if Section 21.1 has wording this doc contradicts, the PRD wins and this file should be corrected.*
**Pairs with:** `VALIDATION_PLAN.md` (gates), `templates/INTERVIEW_LOG.md` (one block per interview), `templates/PILOT_TRACKER.md` (counters).

---

## 0. How to use this document

**Length:** 25–30 minutes. Never longer than 35. Nigerians running a business are busy; a long interview gets polite answers, not true ones.

**Format:** Voice note or call first, in person second, chat third. Chat interviews produce short answers and no frustration in the voice — you lose the best signal.

**The one rule:** ask about **the past**, not the future. "How did you handle the last three orders?" beats "Would you use an app that…". Every "would you" answer is worth almost nothing until they've committed to something real.

**Language:** Speak the way they speak. If they mix Pidgin, mix it back. Suggested Pidgin alternatives are in brackets — use them only if the conversation is already going that way, never to perform.

**Do not reveal the product early.** In Sections A–C you are a researcher, not a founder. Say "I'm studying how [business category] businesses handle orders and money" — not "we're building an app". If they ask directly, say: *"I'll show you what I'm working on at the end — I don't want to lead your answers before then."* That is honest and almost always accepted.

**Target market:** Nigerian businesses of all kinds and sizes — not just fashion, accessories or boutiques. The sample should cover at least four categories (for example retail, fashion, electronics, food, beauty, household goods, wholesale/distribution, services). Record each participant's **pillar fit** — business management, marketplace, both, or unclear. Do not assume every business needs every feature.

**Consent, before you start recording:**
> "I'll take notes while we talk — is that okay? I won't post your name, your customers' names, or your prices anywhere."

If they say no, take mental notes and write them up within 10 minutes.

**What to capture verbatim:** the exact words they use for the pain ("I dey always forget", "e dey stress me", "I just dey guess"). Their vocabulary is the copy for the product later.

**Signal discipline:** at the end of each interview, score it strong / weak / none in the log. A warm "this is a very good idea, God bless you" with no commitment is **none**. Log it as none. Do not round up.

## 1. BUSINESS OWNERS

*Business owners across industries — retail, fashion, electronics, food, beauty, household goods, wholesale/distribution, services and other sectors — who take orders through WhatsApp DMs, status, Instagram, phone, in person, or a mix. They already sell, and many already lose money to forgotten balances.*

**Target profile:** any Nigerian business that takes orders and tracks payments, from owner-run shops to larger teams. 5–40 orders a week is a recruiting guide, not a filter. Sells on credit or "half payment now" at least sometimes. Record whether the business needs business management, marketplace, or both.

### A. Introduction & Framing (≈3 min)

**Open:**
> "Good afternoon ma/sir. My name is ____. I'm doing a small study on how business owners like you keep track of orders and customers who still owe — the everyday way you people dey manage am. It's not a sales call, I'm not selling anything today. I just want to learn from someone who actually does it.
>
> It'll take about 20–25 minutes. There's no right or wrong answer — if something I ask doesn't apply to your business, just tell me and we'll skip it.
>
> One thing: I'll ask you to show me how you currently do it — your notebook, your chat, your Excel, anything. Only if you're comfortable. You can hide customer names.
>
> Is it okay if I take notes?"

**If they ask "are you building an app?":**
> "I'm looking at whether this problem is worth solving at all. That's why I'm asking before I build anything. At the end I'll show you a rough idea and you tell me if it's nonsense."

**Why this framing:** it gives them status (they're the expert), removes sales pressure, and earns the right to ask for their real records later.

### B. Warm-Up Questions (≈5 min)

Goal: understand the physical setup of the business before touching pain.

1. "So tell me about the business — what do you sell, and how long have you been doing it?"
2. "On a normal day, how do customers reach you? WhatsApp, Instagram, walk-in, referrals — which one brings the most orders?" *(Listen: do they name one channel or five? Five channels = five places to lose an order.)*
3. "Roughly how many orders do you handle in a week? And what's the biggest order value you've handled?"
4. "Is it just you, or does somebody help you — sister, sales girl, brother?"
5. "Show me your phone for a second — which apps do you open first in the morning?" *(Observe, don't just ask. Note the order: WhatsApp, then Instagram, then bank app? That order is their real workflow.)*
6. "Do you use WhatsApp Business or normal WhatsApp? Have you set up the catalogue and the quick replies?"
7. "When a customer says 'I'm coming to pick it up later' — where does that order live until they come?"

**Interviewer notes:**
- Q5 and Q7 are the highest-value warm-ups. Q7 usually produces the first honest sigh.
- If they say "everything is in my head" — do not react. Just say "okay, and how does that work when you're tired or busy?" and let them talk.

### C. Core Problem Deep-Dive (≈10 min)

Goal: reconstruct real events. Do not accept summaries.

**C1 — The last order, end to end**
> "Let's take the last order you delivered — not a typical one, the actual last one. Walk me through it from the first message. What did the customer say first?"

Then follow the thread with these probes, in this order:
- "Where did you write it down?"
- "Did they pay everything, half, or nothing?"
- "How did you know they had paid — did you check your bank alert, or they sent a screenshot?"
- "What did you do next?"
- "And after that?"

Repeat with **two more** orders if time allows. Ask for one **fully paid** and one **part-paid**. The part-paid one is where the script earns its money.

**C2 — The bookkeeping**
- "Show me where you're tracking these orders right now." *(Then stay quiet. Watch what they open. If it's a notebook, ask to see the page. If it's a chat, ask them to scroll to the last customer with a balance.)*
- "Is this the same place you keep what they've paid, or is that somewhere else?"
- "If I asked you right now, 'how much has this customer paid so far and what's remaining?' how long would it take you to answer?" *(Time it. Say the number back: "so about 40 seconds.")*
- "What happens when the notebook is not with you? Or when your phone is dead?"

**C3 — Deposits and installments**
- "Do customers usually pay everything before you deliver, or do they drop something first?"
- "When somebody pays in two or three parts — how do you record the second payment? Does the first one still show?"
- "Has there been a time when you recorded a part payment and later couldn't tell how much was left? Tell me about that one."
- "Do you ever give credit — 'take it, pay me later'? To who, and why them?"

**C4 — Promises to pay**
- "Customers say 'I'll send it on Friday' — when that happens, where does that promise go?"
- "How do you remember to check on Friday?"
- "Tell me about the last time somebody promised and didn't pay. What did you do? How many times did you follow up?"
- "How does it feel to chase somebody for money?" *(This is the emotional core. Let the silence sit. Do not rescue them.)*
- "Have you ever just… let it go? How much was it?" *(Write the amount down. This is your price anchor.)*

**C5 — The cost**
- "In the last month, how much money would you say you lost or forgot to collect?" *(If they can't answer: "just a rough guess, even if it's small.")*
- "How many customers owe you money **right now**?" *(Watch how they answer. Notebook? Scrolling chats? Guessing? Their method is your competitor.)*

**Listen for (unprompted, in their words) — tick these in the log:**
- [ ] Loss of money (forgotten balance, under-collected, given out twice)
- [ ] Time wasted searching chats
- [ ] Embarrassment / awkwardness of chasing
- [ ] Trust problem with staff ("my sales girl dey forget")
- [ ] Records lost — phone spoilt, notebook finished, WhatsApp cleared
- [ ] Confusion when a customer disputes what they paid

**Red flags in this section:**
- They never give credit and everything is prepaid → weak fit for the debt promise.
- Volume under ~3 orders a week → the pain isn't recurring enough to build a habit.
- They already use a tool they're happy with → ask what it does well before assuming there's a gap.

### D. Conceptual Solution & Workflow Feedback (≈5 min)

**Reveal — keep it verbal and plain, no jargon:**
> "Let me show you the idea I'm testing, then I want you to attack it.
>
> The idea is one simple list: every customer who still owes you, with the amount. When you tap a name, it opens WhatsApp with a message already written — 'Good afternoon ma, this is a reminder about the balance of ₦12,000 on your order' — and you can edit it before you send. Nothing sends by itself.
>
> When you add an order, you also record when they pay small small, so the history stays there. And at the end you can send them a small receipt as a picture.
>
> That's it. No bank connection, everything stays on your phone."

**Then — feedback, one question at a time:**
1. "Say that back to me — what do you understand it to do?" *(Reveals the gap between what you said and what they heard. Usually large.)*
2. "What part of that would actually help you tomorrow morning?"
3. "What part would you never use?"
4. "If this was on your phone today, when exactly would you open it — before you sleep, when a customer chats, when you're owing a supplier?"
5. "What would make you stop using it after one week?"
6. "How would you feel about typing your customer names and amounts into it? Any worry?" *(Data trust matters here — be honest that it stays on the phone and can be lost if the phone is lost or cleared.)*
7. "Where should the reminder message come from — your normal number, or a business number?" *(Reveals whether they fear looking like a debt collector.)*
8. "If we charged for this — small money, once, not monthly — what would make it worth it to you? And what would make it too much?"

**Do not defend the idea.** If they list five problems, say "that's useful", write them down, and move on. Defending kills the next honest sentence.

### E. Closing & Commitment Request (≈4 min)

Ask for exactly **one** thing, in this order. Offer the next one only if the first is declined.

**Ask A (strongest, use first):**
> "I'm going to test this with a few business owners for 7 days. Would you be one of them? It means entering your real orders for one week — 5 minutes a day. If I set you up on ____ (name a real date), can you start that day?"

*Then: confirm the date, confirm the channel (WhatsApp), and send a calendar-style follow-up message before you leave. A "yes" without a date is a no.*

**Ask B:**
> "Can I see one page of how you currently track — a photo of the notebook page, or a screenshot of your own tracker? You can cover the customer names."

**Ask C (weakest but still real):**
> "Would you be open to me showing you a rough version next week and you tell me if I got it wrong? 10 minutes."

**Then thank them properly:**
> "Thank you — this is genuinely useful. I'll send you a short message tomorrow so you have my number. And if I build this, you'll be the first to see it."

**Log immediately, before the next interview:** quotes, the amount they lost, how they answered "who owes me now", ask outcome + date, signal strength.

## 2. SUPPLIERS / PRODUCT OWNERS

*Wholesalers, importers, manufacturers, and product owners who sell through resellers. Their business is volume through other people — so their problems are visibility, price control, and trust.*

**Target profile:** supplies 5+ resellers, has a price list that resellers should not resell at cost to their own customers, and fulfils orders either directly or through the reseller.

### A. Introduction & Framing (≈3 min)

**Open:**
> "Good afternoon. My name is ____. I'm studying how suppliers and product owners manage their resellers — how orders come in, how you confirm stock, and how you protect your prices. Not a sales call; I want to learn how it actually works from someone doing it.
>
> About 25 minutes. If a question doesn't apply, just wave it off and we'll move.
>
> I'd also like to see how you currently share prices and confirm stock — a screenshot is fine, and you can blur anything you don't want me to see.
>
> Can I take notes?"

**If they're guarded about prices** (common — price lists are their competitive asset):
> "You don't need to tell me your actual costs or margins. I only care about the *process* — who sees the price, how they see it, and what goes wrong. Blur the numbers if you like."

**Why this framing matters:** suppliers assume anyone asking about resellers is trying to poach them or sell them software. Naming their fear out loud ("price protection") buys you honesty.

### B. Warm-Up Questions (≈5 min)

1. "Tell me about the business — what do you supply, and how long have you been doing it?"
2. "Who sells for you? Are they formal resellers you know by name, or anybody who picks up from you?"
3. "Roughly how many active resellers do you have in a month? How many are consistent?"
4. "How does a reseller find out your current price — do you send a list, do they ask each time, is it on your status?"
5. "How do they place an order with you? WhatsApp, call, they come to the shop?"
6. "Who handles delivery — you, or the reseller?"
7. "How many people work with you on this — do you have someone answering the reseller chats?"

**Interviewer notes:**
- Q4 and Q5 set up the entire deep-dive. Note the *medium* of the price list: PDF, image, voice note, memory. Images get forwarded; that's the leak.
- If they have under 3 resellers, this persona's pain is probably not strong enough yet — say so in the log.

### C. Core Problem Deep-Dive (≈10 min)

**C1 — Price privacy and leakage**
- "Walk me through the last time you sent out a new price list. What did you send — image, PDF, typed message? Who did you send it to?"
- "Have you ever found a reseller selling below your price, or reselling to another reseller at cost? What happened?"
- "When that happened, how did you find out? Did someone tell you, or did you see it yourself?"
- "What do you do about it now — do you confront them, cut them off, ignore it?"
- "Do your resellers know each other's prices? Do they know what you charge another reseller?"
- "If a reseller had a link they could open to see *their own* price, and only their own — would that change anything for you? What would worry you about it?"

**C2 — Stock confirmations**
- "When a reseller asks 'is this still available?', how do you confirm it?"
- "How many times a day does that question come in?"
- "What happens when you say yes and it's actually finished — has that happened recently? Tell me about that one."
- "Do you keep a stock count anywhere? Or is it 'I know what's in the store'?"
- "When stock is low, how do your resellers find out — do you announce it, or do they find out by asking?"

**C3 — Orders and fulfilment**
- "Take the last order a reseller placed. How did it come in, and what did you do, step by step?"
- "Where do you write it down? Do you write it down at all?"
- "How do you know what you've dispatched and what you haven't?"
- "Have you ever sent the wrong quantity, or sent to the wrong reseller? What did it cost you?"
- "Who pays delivery? Has that ever caused a fight?"
- "When a reseller says 'I sent the money' — how do you verify before you release goods?"

**C4 — Trust and the reseller relationship**
- "What's the hardest part of working with resellers — is it the money, the stock, or the attitude?" *(Their ranking is the product priority.)*
- "Have you ever stopped supplying someone? Why?"
- "Do resellers ever go behind you and buy directly from your own supplier? How do you protect against that?"

**Listen for (unprompted):**
- [ ] Price list leaking / resellers undercutting each other
- [ ] Being asked "is it available" ten times a day
- [ ] Overselling stock they don't have
- [ ] No record of what was dispatched to whom
- [ ] Resellers disputing what they ordered
- [ ] Resellers taking their customer relationships

**Red flags:**
- They don't care about price privacy ("everybody knows my price") → the privacy feature has no value to them; check whether resellers care instead.
- They physically hand goods to 2 people only → the coordination problem doesn't exist yet.

### D. Conceptual Solution & Workflow Feedback (≈5 min)

**Reveal:**
> "Here's what I'm testing. Two parts.
>
> One: you put your products in once, with the price you want each reseller to see. When a reseller opens their link, they see **their** price — not your cost, not another reseller's price. You decide who sees what.
>
> Two: when they order, it comes to you in one list — who ordered, what quantity, what they've paid, and whether it has been dispatched. Instead of scrolling twenty chats to find out what's pending.
>
> Stock status you update once, and everybody sees the same thing."

**Feedback questions:**
1. "What's the first thing that worries you about that?"
2. "Would your resellers actually use a link, or would they still just message you on WhatsApp? Be honest." *(This is the killer question. Most will say they'd still message. Write down the reason.)*
3. "If a reseller orders through a link, do you trust that order the same way as a WhatsApp message? What's missing?"
4. "Should your resellers be able to see each other at all — even just names?"
5. "Should a reseller be able to set their own price for their own customer, higher than yours? Would you want to see what price they set?"
6. "What must this thing **never** show, for you to be comfortable?"
7. "Would you pay for this, or do you expect it free because it's helping you sell?" *(Suppliers often expect reseller tools free. Get their answer now, not later.)*

### E. Closing & Commitment Request (≈4 min)

**Ask A (strongest):**
> "I want to test the ordering side with one real supplier. Would you let me set up 5 of your products, and give the link to **one** reseller you trust, for one week? No cost, and you can stop it any time. If yes, which reseller, and which 5 products?"

*If they hesitate on the reseller:* "Then just the 5 products and your price list format — I'll build it and show you before anyone sees it."

**Ask B:**
> "Can you send me your current price list the way you send it to resellers — image, PDF, or the message itself? Blur whatever you want."

**Ask C:**
> "Can I call you again in one week, after I've shown this to a few resellers, and ask whether their reaction matches what you expected?"

**Close:**
> "Thank you. I'll send you the setup before ____ so you can see exactly what your reseller would see."

## 3. EMERGING VENDORS / RESELLERS

*People who sell products they don't own. They source from a supplier or a bigger vendor, add a margin, advertise on WhatsApp status and Instagram, and either drop-ship or hold small stock.*

**Target profile:** 1–3 years selling, sources from at least one regular supplier, has an informal commission or markup arrangement, not yet registered as a business.

### A. Introduction & Framing (≈3 min)

**Open:**
> "Hi, thank you for your time. My name is ____. I'm studying how vendors like you source products and sell them — how you get the goods, how you agree your own price, and how you deliver to customers. It's research, I'm not selling anything.
>
> About 20–25 minutes. No wrong answers — if something doesn't apply to you, just say so.
>
> I'd like to see how you currently get product photos and prices from your supplier — screenshots are fine, blur any name you want.
>
> Can I take notes?"

**Tone note:** this group is the most price-sensitive and the most likely to feel judged ("you're not a real business"). Be warm, never condescending, and never call their arrangement "informal" to their face.

### B. Warm-Up Questions (≈5 min)

1. "What do you sell, and how did you start?"
2. "Where do your products come from — one supplier, or several? How did you find them?"
3. "On a normal week, how many orders do you get? Where do they come from — status, Instagram, referrals?"
4. "Is this your main income, or something on the side?"
5. "Who else does this in your circle — do you people compare notes on suppliers?"
6. "When you need a new product to sell, what do you actually do first?" *(Listen: do they scroll a supplier's status, ask in a group, or wait to be told?)*

### C. Core Problem Deep-Dive (≈10 min)

**C1 — Sourcing**
- "Take the last product you started selling. How did you find it, and what did you have to do before you could advertise it?"
- "Who gave you the price — the supplier directly, or a middleman? Did you negotiate?"
- "How long did it take from 'I want to sell this' to 'I posted it'?"
- "What do you do when the supplier's price changes? How do you find out, and how do you update your own price?"
- "Has a supplier changed price on you after you'd already quoted a customer? What happened?"

**C2 — Commission and margin agreements**
- "How do you make your money — you buy and resell at your own price, or the supplier pays you a commission?"
- "If it's commission: how do you know what you're owed? Where is it written?"
- "Has a supplier ever shorted your commission, or 'forgotten' an order was yours? Tell me about that."
- "Do you have anything in writing with any supplier — chat agreement counts?"
- "Who sets the minimum price you can sell at? Can you sell higher?"

**C3 — Advertising permission**
- "When you post a product, whose photos do you use?"
- "Did the supplier say you could use their photos and videos? Or did you just take them?"
- "Has a supplier ever complained that you used their content, or that you priced too low?"
- "Do you make your own content — videos, voice notes, your face?"
- "What happens when two resellers post the same product with the same photos at different prices? Does that affect you?"

**C4 — Fulfilment and the customer relationship**
- "When a customer pays you, what happens next — do you send to the supplier, or you already have the item?"
- "Who delivers to the final customer?"
- "If the item is finished after the customer has paid — what do you do? Has that happened?"
- "Does your supplier know your customer's name and number? Do you want them to?"
- "When the customer has a complaint, who do they call — you or the supplier?"
- "Have you ever lost a customer because the supplier delayed or sent the wrong thing?"

**C5 — Money flow**
- "Does the customer pay you first, or pay the supplier directly?"
- "If they pay you, and you have to pay the supplier before delivery — what does that do to your cash?"
- "When the supplier owes you (commission or refund), how do you track it?"

**Listen for (unprompted):**
- [ ] Supplier changing prices after quoting
- [ ] Not knowing what commission is owed
- [ ] Fear of losing a customer to the supplier
- [ ] No permission to use photos, or content stolen and reused by others
- [ ] Stock finishing after customer has paid
- [ ] Being blamed by the customer for the supplier's delay

**Red flags:**
- They hold all their own stock and set all their own prices → they're closer to Persona 1 than Persona 3.
- They sell one product from one supplier who is a family member → the coordination pain is absorbed by the relationship.

### D. Conceptual Solution & Workflow Feedback (≈5 min)

**Reveal:**
> "What I'm testing: a supplier puts their products in with the price *they* want you to see. You get your own link — like a small shop with your name on it. You set your own selling price on top, and your customer sees **your** price and **your** name, not the supplier's.
>
> When a customer orders, the order goes to the supplier for fulfilment, but it's marked as yours — so your commission or your margin is recorded and you can see what you're owed.
>
> And you'd only see products the supplier has allowed you to sell, at the price they've allowed."

**Feedback questions:**
1. "What's the first thing you like, and the first thing you don't trust?"
2. "If your customer opened that link, would they know it's not your own shop? Does that matter to you?"
3. "Would you be comfortable with the supplier seeing your customer's name and number? What if they could see the order but not the contact?" *(This is the crux. Their answer decides the data model.)*
4. "Would you set your price higher than the supplier's minimum? What would stop you?"
5. "If the supplier updates their price, should your price change automatically, or should you approve it first?"
6. "How would you want to be paid — customer pays you directly, or pays the platform and you get your cut later?"
7. "What would make you abandon this after one week?"

**Do not oversell the "your own shop" idea.** Many will say it sounds good and then never send the link to a customer. Ask: "would you send this to a customer who already trusts you, or only to new ones?" Their answer predicts real use.

### E. Closing & Commitment Request (≈4 min)

**Ask A (strongest):**
> "Would you let me set up a small version with 3 products from your main supplier, and send it to **one real customer** this week? Even one order is enough for me to learn. What day can you do it?"

*If the supplier hasn't agreed:* "Then let's do it with 3 products and I'll blur the supplier's name — I want to see whether a customer will actually buy from a link like that."

**Ask B:**
> "Can you send me one product you're currently advertising — the photo, your price, and what the supplier charges you (blur the number if you prefer)? I want to see how you present it."

**Ask C:**
> "Can I check back in a week and ask how many orders came through the link?"

**Close:**
> "Thank you, this was helpful. I'll message you so you have my number — and whatever we set up, you can stop it any time."

## 4. Appendix

### 4.1 Universal probes (use when an answer is thin)
- "Tell me more about that."
- "What happened next?"
- "Why did you do it that way and not the other way?"
- "What did that cost you — money, time, or the customer?"
- "When was the last time? Not generally — that specific time."
- "Show me." *(The single strongest probe in this whole document.)*
- "And how did that make you feel?" *(Only once per interview; overuse feels like therapy.)*

### 4.2 Phrases to avoid
| Avoid | Say instead |
|---|---|
| "Would you use an app that…" | "How do you handle it now?" |
| "Don't you think it's stressful?" | "How does that work for you?" |
| "Most sellers tell me…" | "Some people mention… does that happen to you?" |
| "So you'd pay ₦5,000 for this?" | "What do you pay for any tool today, and how much?" |
| "That's a great idea" | "That's useful — why do you say that?" |
| "You should try…" | Say nothing. Write it down. |

### 4.3 Handling the three most common objections

**"Just send me the app, I'll test it."** → "I will, but I don't want to waste your time on something half-built. Let me finish the first version — can I put you down for the week of ____?" *(Then actually follow up on that date.)*

**"Is this not what WhatsApp Business catalogue does?"** → "It does some of it. What does it *not* do for you?" *(Their answer is competitive intelligence. Never argue that your idea is better.)*

**"Are you going to charge me later?"** → "If I charge anything, it will be small and one-time, and I'll tell you before, not after. Right now I'm only learning whether this is useful at all."

### 4.4 Commitment ladder (same as `VALIDATION_PLAN.md`)
- **A** = a date to use the real thing. Counts as a commitment.
- **B** = they hand over real artifacts (notebook photo, price list, ad, screenshot).
- **C** = a scheduled follow-up.
- Anything else, including enthusiasm, = **no signal**.

Only **A or B** counts toward the gate ("3 sellers accepted ask A or B").

### 4.5 After the interview (5 minutes, do not skip)
1. Fill one block in the canonical `INTERVIEW_LOG.md` — especially the **exact quotes**, the **amount lost**, and the **pillar fit**.
2. Update the counters in the canonical `PILOT_TRACKER.md`: interviews completed, category coverage, same problem named unprompted, commitments.
3. Write one line: *"What surprised me was ____."* If nothing surprised you, you probably asked leading questions.
4. Send the thank-you message the same day, with your name in it, so they can find you again.

### 4.6 Fairness and safety
- Never promise money, a job, or free goods. Airtime as a thank-you is fine if you can afford it, and must not be framed as payment for a positive answer.
- Never publish a real customer name, phone number, price list, or business photo without written permission in the chat.
- Do not record video calls. Do not screenshot their customers' chats.
- If they ask you to keep something off the record, keep it off the record — including in your own notes.
- Interview in their language, at their pace. If they're serving a customer mid-interview, wait, and use it as an observation.

### 4.7 Category diversity and pillar fit
The gate in `VALIDATION_PLAN.md` requires **at least 8 interviews across at least 4 business categories**. These three scripts exist so you can test the three-sided model and both product pillars — not so you can count three half-segments as progress. Do not narrow the market to one vertical: spread interviews across categories, keep any single category below roughly half the sample, and record each participant's pillar fit (business management, marketplace, both, or unclear). A business that needs only one pillar is valid evidence, not a failed interview.

### 4.8 Pillar-fit questions (ask every participant)
1. "Which is the bigger headache today — keeping track of customers, orders and money, or finding and managing suppliers and resellers?"
2. "If you could fix only one, which would you fix first?"
3. "Do you ever sell other people's products, or supply products to people who sell them?"
4. "Do you need both sides to work together, or would one side alone be useful?"
5. "Which part of what I described would you never use?"

Record `BUSINESS_MANAGEMENT`, `MARKETPLACE`, `BOTH`, or `UNCLEAR`. Do not mark `BOTH` just because both features exist.
